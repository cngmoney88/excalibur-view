Move sheets around in Thumbnails and drop PDFs into a drawing, change a drawing's own words, reshape markups after they're down, Cloud+, your signature, and a Classic look.

MOVING SHEETS IN THUMBNAILS

Drag a sheet up or down the Thumbnails list and let go where it belongs. A
bar shows where it will land. Picked sheets move together: Ctrl-click a few,
then drag any one of them. Near the top or bottom of the list it scrolls to
follow.

Drag a PDF from File Explorer or the Finder onto the list and its sheets go into the
drawing where you let go. Anywhere else on the window, a dropped PDF still
opens in a tab of its own.

Right-click a sheet for the rest: Insert Pages from a File, Insert a Blank
Sheet, Move to the Top or the Bottom, Rotate Right or Left, and Delete.

All of it changes the drawing itself, and Undo puts it back, one step at a
time. Markups move with their sheets. Bookmarks, layers and everything else
in the file are left as they were.

A set locked so its sheets can't be changed, one that opens with a
password, and a drawing shared through the office server say so rather than
moving anything. For the office server's drawings, Save As makes a copy you
can rearrange.

EDITING THE DRAWING'S WORDS

Edit -> Edit Text outlines every paragraph on the sheet. Click one and
retype it where it is. The strip above it sets the size, colour, bold,
italic and alignment. Done or Ctrl+Enter writes it into the drawing, and
Esc or Cancel leaves it as it was.

The lines of a note are treated as one paragraph and wrap to the width the
note already had. The next numbered note stays a paragraph of its own.

The words keep the drawing's own font when it has every letter they need.
Many PDFs only carry the letters the drawing already used, so a new letter
sends the paragraph to the nearest standard font, and the status line says
which one and why.

Before the change is kept, the rest of the sheet is drawn again and has to
come out exactly as it was. If it doesn't, nothing is written. Undo puts
the words back.

Lettering that a CAD program drew as lines isn't text and can't be retyped.
For those, a white-filled box over the old words and a text box on top
does the job.

MARKUPS AFTER THEY'RE PLACED

Pick a markup and it shows handles. Drag a corner or a side to resize it,
drag a point to move just that point, and drag a callout's tip to aim its
leader somewhere else. Pictures and signatures keep their shape unless you
hold Shift.

Double-click a text box or a callout to retype it on the sheet.

Boxes, circles and clouds now stay the size they were drawn. Before, each
redraw could make them a little bigger.

Stamps, pictures and signatures that came in with a file keep their look
when you move or resize them.

A moved markup no longer leaves a faint copy behind where it was.

FILL, OUTLINE AND BLEND

Right-click a markup for its fill, outline, line weight and see-through,
with any colour you like. Multiply blends a fill into the drawing the way
a highlighter does, so the linework under it still shows. The pen chooser
has the same.

CLOUD+

Cloud+ is on the Markup menu and the text toolbar. Drag out the cloud,
click where the words go, and type. A leader runs from the cloud to the
words.

The cloud and its words move, copy and delete as one. Drag the words box
by itself to put it somewhere else and the leader follows. Double-click
the cloud to change the words. Other PDF programs see an ordinary cloud
and callout, grouped together.

YOUR SIGNATURE

Markup -> Signature. The first time, draw it with the mouse or a pen, or
load a photo or scan of it on paper and the paper is taken out. It is kept
on this computer, so after that it's one click. Initials work the same
way, and you can keep more than one.

A signature goes down as a stamp that blends into the drawing, so the
lines under it still show. Move and resize it like any other markup.

CLASSIC LOOK

View -> Classic Look turns the program grey, with raised buttons, sunken
boxes and lists, and a navy band across the window in front, the way office
computers looked in the late nineties. Everything works the same; only the
look changes. It is remembered for next time, and Edit -> Preferences has it
too.

FIXED

Pasting a box, circle, cloud, stamp or text box could leave a thin sliver
instead of the shape you copied. Pasted markups now keep their size, a
callout's leader comes with it, and a pasted Cloud+ is a new Cloud+ of its
own.

A save that only changed markups already in the file could leave it saying
it held fewer objects than it did. Excalibur View read past that, but
stricter PDF readers could lose part of the file. Saves now count every
object.

A long message at the bottom of the window is cut to fit instead of
running over the sheet name. Point at it to read all of it.

A set locked with an owner's password, the way most engineers send them,
read back scrambled after a save until it was opened again, and a second
save in the same sitting could spoil the markups it wrote. It stays
readable now.

If another seat moves a shared drawing's sheets while you have it open,
your next save says so instead of putting your markups on the wrong sheets.
